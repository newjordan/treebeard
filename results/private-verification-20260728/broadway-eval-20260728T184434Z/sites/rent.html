Overview

RENT is a rock musical based on Giacomo Puccini's opera La Bohème. Set in the East Village of New York City in 1991 (though the show premiered in 1996), it follows a group of artists struggling to survive and create art amidst the HIV/AIDS epidemic, gentrification, and the crack epidemic.

Jonathan Larson

Jonathan Larson (1960–1996) was an American composer, lyricist, and playwright who spent over a decade writing RENT. He famously refused to take a day off, a habit that tragically contributed to his death from an aortic dissection just 25 hours after the Broadway premiere.

Larson's vision was to make opera relevant to a new generation, using rock music to tell stories about the marginalized. He died before seeing the massive cultural impact of his work.

Story & Themes

Adapted from Puccini's La Bohème, the story centers on Mark Cohen, a struggling filmmaker, and his roommate Roger Davis, a former punk rock star and HIV-positive composer. They are joined by their friends: Mimi, a exotic dancer and drug addict; Maureen, a performance artist; Tom Collins, a former anarchist professor; Angel, a street youth; and Joanne, Maureen's girlfriend.


  • HIV/AIDS: The show was groundbreaking in its honest depiction of the epidemic, focusing on the human cost rather than just the politics.
  • Community vs. Gentrification: The threat of developers buying out the neighborhood looms over the characters' lives.
  • Art as Survival: The characters use their art to process trauma, love, and loss.
  • Chosen Family: In the absence of biological family, the group forms a tight-knit, supportive unit.

RENT swept the 1996 awards season. It won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama and four Tony Awards, including Best Musical. It also won the Drama Desk Award for Outstanding Musical and the Outer Critics Circle Award for Best Musical.

Legacy

"Don't daydream, be here."

RENT changed the landscape of Broadway. It brought in a younger, more diverse audience and proved that rock music could carry a massive theatrical production. It sparked crucial conversations about HIV/AIDS awareness and LGBTQ+ rights in mainstream media.

The annual "Rent-a-thon" performances on New Year's Eve have become a tradition, where thousands of performers across the globe perform the show simultaneously to raise money for HIV/AIDS charities.
