The Phantom of the Opera

Music by Andrew Lloyd Webber

Overview

Based on the French novel by Gaston Leroux, The Phantom of the Opera is the story of a mysterious, disfigured musical genius who lives in the depths of the Paris Opera House. He becomes obsessively in love with the beautiful young soprano, Christine Daaé.

Premiering on October 9, 1986, at the Her Majesty's Theatre in London, and subsequently opening on Broadway in January 1988, the show became the defining mega-musical of the era. It shattered box office records and set a new standard for theatrical scale and spectacle.

Creative Team

Music & Lyrics

Andrew Lloyd Webber

Webber composed a through-sung score (no spoken dialogue) that blended rock, pop, and classical styles, creating a timeless romantic atmosphere.

Direction & Choreography

Sir Hal Prince / Michael Bennett

Hal Prince took over direction for the Broadway production, bringing his immense experience in pacing and spectacle. The choreography, originally by Michael Bennett, utilized the architecture of the stage to create haunting, dreamlike sequences.

Book

Richard Stilgoe & Andrew Lloyd Webber

The Story

The narrative follows the "Phantom," a deformed musical prodigy living in the catacombs beneath the Paris Opéra. He acts as a secret guardian to Christine Daaé, a chorus girl with a beautiful voice.

Christine's romantic feelings are divided between the Phantom and the young, wealthy Viscount Raoul de Chagny. The Phantom's jealousy leads to a dramatic confrontation involving a chandelier, a subterranean boat, and the famous "Masquerade" ball. Ultimately, the Phantom's humanity is revealed through his love for Christine, leading to his final release.

Iconic Design

The production is famous for its massive scale. The centerpiece is the giant crystal chandelier (weighing over 400kg) that crashes down during the "Masquerade" act. The set design by John Napier features a massive, working pipe organ and a revolving stage that reveals the Phantom's underground lair.

Long-Run Record & Cultural Impact

The show holds the record for the longest-running show in Broadway history. It ran for 35 years, closing on January 10, 2023, after 13,981 performances.

It was the first show in history to gross $1 billion. Its cultural impact is immeasurable, spawning a global franchise, a 2004 film adaptation directed by Joel Schumacher, and countless regional productions. It defined the "Mega-Musical" era alongside shows like Cats and Sunset Boulevard.
