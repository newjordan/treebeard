HAMILTON

The Story of Today's America

Overview

Premiering in 2015, Lin-Manuel Miranda's Hamilton is a sung-and-rapped-through biographical musical that tells the story of American founding father Alexander Hamilton.

What began as an Off-Broadway sensation at The Public Theater quickly moved to Broadway's Richard Rodgers Theatre, becoming a global cultural phenomenon. It redefined the musical theater landscape by utilizing hip-hop, R&B, and traditional show tunes to tell the story of America's past through a diverse cast.

Story Approach

"Old York, New York." The narrative follows Alexander Hamilton from his impoverished childhood in the Caribbean to his pivotal role in the Revolutionary War, the drafting of the Constitution, and his tumultuous political career.

The show focuses heavily on the theme of legacy—who lives, who dies, and who tells your story. It humanizes the historical figures, portraying them as flawed, ambitious young men and women.

The Founding Fathers

Cast by color to reflect the diversity of the current United States, emphasizing that the story belongs to everyone.

Historical Context

Interweaves the Federalist Papers, the duel with Aaron Burr, and the personal tragedies of Eliza and Angelica Schuyler.

Musical Style

Hip-hop, R&B, Pop, and Traditional Showtunes.

Miranda utilizes hip-hop as a vehicle for political debate and rapid-fire exposition. The score is a fusion of genres that mirrors the melting pot of the characters.

Cultural Phenomenon

Hamilton did more than break box office records; it changed the demographic of theater audiences. The "Hamilton Effect" saw a massive surge in interest in American history, particularly among younger generations.

The release of the filmed stage production on Disney+ in 2020 further cemented its place in pop culture history, making it accessible to millions worldwide.
